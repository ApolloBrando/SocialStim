# 2. DAY-TO-DAY GAMEPLAY: COPY GNOSIA'S STRUCTURE
Replace the current day structure with a Gnosia-style loop. If you can browse, consult Gnosia references to match its pacing and feel as closely as you can. Core facts to replicate:
- Each day is a group DISCUSSION made of several rounds (default 5, configurable 3 to 7). Each round is a sequence of NPC statements and exchanges. The player gets at least one "player window" per round (configurable 1 to 2) in which they can act. They may also simply let the round pass.
- NPCs react to each other and to the player: accusations get answered, defenses get challenged, agreement chains and pile-ons happen. When someone directly accuses or questions the player, the player gets a priority "Respond" prompt (see section 4).
- After the final round comes a VOTE. Every character votes. Reveal votes one by one for tension, show the tally, and the plurality target is eliminated (this replaces the nomination > defense > judgement trial as the default; keep the existing trial flow only as an optional "Trial Mode" voting setting if it already works, otherwise don't spend time on it). Handle ties (configurable: one revote, then no elimination) and abstentions (configurable).
- One elimination per day, then a role reveal for the eliminated character (configurable on/off) and last words.
- FREE TIME (optional, toggleable in settings; implement after the core is solid): in Gnosia you explore the ship and talk to individual characters after the debates. Text-only equivalent: after the vote, the player picks a character (or a location) to spend time with, 1 to 2 actions per day. This builds the relationship, may trigger private exchanges (a private tip, a private claim, or, for evil players, secret coordination with teammates), and may carry risk or information. NPCs do the same in the background, so some of their relationships and knowledge shift off-screen.
- NIGHT: roles with night abilities choose targets. The player uses a clean target menu, and NPCs decide via AI. Resolve actions through the priority pipeline (section 5). Then a MORNING REPORT: who died, how it's phrased, and which results/notices each player receives.
- Per-run character STATS, like Gnosia: Charisma, Intuition, Logic, Charm, Performance, Stealth. They affect how strongly someone's statements sway others, how well-reasoned statements land, inference accuracy, relationship gain, how believable a lie is, and how likely someone is to be suspected/targeted. They are generated per run (seeded) for every character, including the player, and optionally point-buy for the player at setup (setting: Random / Balanced / Point-buy). Stats NEVER persist between runs. Some player dialogue options may be stat-gated (e.g. "[Logic 6+]").
- Relationships/affinity between characters (bonds and grudges) that evolve and influence votes, defenses, and who believes whom.

Remember: no time loops, no cross-run progression, every run self-contained.

# 4. INTERACTION SYSTEM (the current action system is CLUTTERED; replace it entirely)
The core flow is exactly:
   Chat > Target     |     Accuse > Target     |     Defend > Target
- Top level during a player window: exactly three always-visible buttons: CHAT, ACCUSE, DEFEND. Plus contextual buttons that appear ONLY when valid (never greyed-out clutter): CLAIM ROLE (see below) and, when the player is directly addressed or accused, a priority RESPOND prompt that collapses the options into: Defend yourself / Counter-accuse / Claim role / Stay silent. There's also a "Stay silent / Pass" option for letting the round pass.
- Step 1: pick the action. Step 2: pick the target on the roster (only valid targets are selectable; CHAT also offers "Everyone"). Step 3 (phrasing): show up to 3 line options, each with a short tone tag ([Logic], [Gut], [Evidence], [Emotional], [Blunt], [Humor], etc.), sampled from the large dialogue pools and filtered by context and stat gates. Add a "Quick mode" setting that skips step 3 by auto-picking the best-fitting line. Every step has a Back/Cancel. No menus deeper than this.
- CHAT: general conversation with a target or everyone. Line types sampled across categories such as: ask a question, share an observation, agree with a previous statement, disagree with a previous statement, banter, ask for their opinion, change the subject.
- ACCUSE / DEFEND: the line options correspond to REASON types (vote history, claim conflict, behavior, gut feeling, silence, "they defended someone evil", and so on). Whatever reason the player picks is the reason NPCs hear and evaluate. Evil players can pick reasons that are lies, and NPCs can catch contradictions.
- Each action shifts NPC beliefs using the influence model in section 7. Never apply a flat bonus.

## Role claiming rules
- A player (including the player character) whose role has an ACTION (night ability) can CLAIM it truthfully at ANY time they hold the floor: any player window, when directly addressed, and while defending themselves. A claim can include a result report (e.g. a Decryptor reporting a reading, a Physician reporting a save). The player chooses from the results they actually have.
- Role BLUFFING (claiming a role you don't have) is available ONLY to evil roles (and other roles flagged as allowed to bluff in the role data, see section 5). Bluffing can be proactive or a counter-claim against someone who already claimed that role, and a bluffer can attach fabricated results.
- A role with no ability (Solarian) cannot claim anything, as it has no claim button.
- Make this permission data-driven with two per-role flags: CanClaimTruthfully and CanBluff.
- Claims are recorded in the public claims ledger. Duplicate claims of a unique role are an immediate logical conflict that NPC AI must reason about. Changing a claim or contradicting a previous result is allowed (for bluffers) but is heavily penalized in NPC suspicion.
- NPCs use the exact same rules and the same menu logic (they can't claim or bluff when the player couldn't).

# 7. NPC AI (make it actually smart, to your fullest capability)
Goal: NPCs that infer, deceive, coordinate, and make human-feeling mistakes, without ever being omniscient. NPCs only know what their role legitimately grants them. Document the design in docs/AI_DESIGN.md.

## 7.1 Information model
- Public event log (every statement, claim, reported result, vote, death, role reveal, and morning report), private knowledge (own role, teammates for evil, own investigation/heal results), and per-NPC beliefs.
- Beliefs: a probability distribution over roles/factions for every other character (not a single "trust" number), plus a separate credibility score and a relationship/affinity value.
- Role-list knowledge follows the visibility setting. NPCs reason with exactly the information the player would have.

## 7.2 Inference engine
- Constraint/hypothesis reasoning: sample or enumerate role assignments consistent with all public information (claims, results, reveals, deaths, the role list) and weight them by likelihood (determinization / Monte Carlo sampling over consistent worlds, with the sample count tied to difficulty and a performance budget). Marginalize the sampled worlds into beliefs.
- Evidence types with sensible likelihood weights: votes (especially votes for characters later revealed to be Town/Evil), accusations and who made them, defending a later-revealed evil, claims and claim timing, duplicate claims of unique roles, reported results versus ground truth revealed on death, claim contradictions, night death patterns (who the killers chose to kill, who was spared), silence or over-eagerness, and behavior consistency over days.
- Ground truth from role reveals instantly re-weights the past: everyone's earlier votes, defenses, and claims involving that character are re-evaluated.
- Theory of mind: each NPC estimates how suspicious it looks to others and picks defend/deflect/counter-accuse behavior accordingly.

## 7.3 Behavior by faction
- TOWN: share information at good times; power roles claim when threatened, when information is critical, or when a counter-claim forces it. Weigh claim credibility (a duplicate claim means someone lies). Protect likely/confirmed power roles (Physician). Nullifier shoots only above a confidence threshold. Avoid wasting votes, reason about vote math and tie risk, and avoid being the lone voter unless confident.
- EVIL: coordinate as a team (shared night target choice, shared narrative). Targeting prioritizes threats (claimed or likely Decryptor/Physician/Nullifier, strong persuaders) over random picks. Choose plausible bluff roles based on who has already claimed what and the role list. Counter-claim when profitable. Aperture and Singer actions are chosen to support a plan (e.g. frame a Town member, cleanse a teammate, block a likely investigator). "Bus" a teammate only when it clearly helps. Blend into the majority vote, build alibis, avoid obvious patterns, and avoid exposing teammates.
- ANARCHY: kill sparingly enough to avoid exposure, avoid being the vote target, and track the last-survivor endgame math.
- INFINITUM: stay alive, deflect votes, keep a low profile, and subtly benefit from the Evil parity clock (it wants Evil to reach parity while it is alive). Track the parity count explicitly.
- Voting: expected-utility vote choice that includes tally prediction, tie handling, last-vote effects, and the risk of becoming the vote sink.
- Night targeting: expected-value choice using threat assessment and role-list awareness.

## 7.4 Social/persuasion layer
When any character (player or NPC) speaks, other NPCs update beliefs with a weight computed from: speaker credibility and affinity, speaker stats (Charisma, Logic, Performance if lying), the strength of the stated reason, consistency with prior statements, and the listener's traits (gullibility, aggression, loyalty). Relationships evolve from defending, accusing, agreeing, and Free Time. Never use flat bonuses.

## 7.5 Personality and human-ness
Personality traits shape risk tolerance, aggression, trust, talkativeness, bandwagon tendency, and logic skill. Add deliberate bounded imperfection: biases (anchoring, bandwagoning, grudges), occasional mistakes scaled by difficulty, and an in-world tension model (rising paranoia as bodies pile up) that makes NPCs more aggressive and erratic over time. NPCs must feel like individuals and not like clones. A beginner should be able to win and an expert should be challenged.

## 7.6 Difficulty
Easy / Normal / Hard / Brutal. Difficulty changes inference depth, mistake rate, coordination quality, and clue-sharing honesty (not cheating). All difficulties must respect the information rules above.

## 7.7 Link between AI and dialogue (critical)
The AI decides (intent, target, REASON TYPE, truthful vs. deceptive). The dialogue system then selects a pre-made line that matches those tags. A line may only mention a reason that matches the AI's actual reason (or its declared lie, for deceivers). No incoherent lines.

## 7.8 Debugging and balance tooling
- A developer-only debug overlay showing, for any NPC: top beliefs, chosen intent, reason code, and plan.
- A headless simulation harness (EditMode test or batch tool) that runs thousands of AI-only games with chosen settings and reports: win rates by faction, average game length, Infinitum steal rate, role impact, vote accuracy, and crashes or stalled games. Use it to tune the balance table and difficulty. Commit a baseline report to docs/.
- Performance budget: AI computation per turn stays well within a frame budget on the target platform. Sample counts must be configurable.
- Deterministic by seed.
